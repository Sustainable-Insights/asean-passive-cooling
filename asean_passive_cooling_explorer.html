1. The Heat Crisis: Climate Projections & UHI

This section establishes the quantitative baseline for the roadmap. It visualizes the escalating thermal stress across major ASEAN cities up to 2050 under the RCP 4.5 scenario, alongside the compounding effects of Urban Heat Islands (UHI). Understanding these metrics is critical for recognizing the urgency of implementing passive cooling infrastructure to protect vulnerable populations.

📈 Temperature Projections (2000-2050)

Average temperatures are rising across the board, with cities like Bangkok and Kuala Lumpur facing the steepest increases. By 2050, extreme heat days (>35°C) will more than double.

🌆 UHI Intensity vs. Green Cover

Urban Heat Island (UHI) intensity worsens in areas with low green cover. Jakarta and Manila exhibit extreme daytime and nighttime UHI, emphasizing the need for immediate green infrastructure.

⚠

Critical Finding

Bangkok, Ho Chi Minh City, and Manila will experience between 110 and 120 extreme heat days annually by 2050. Projections under RCP 8.5 scenarios predict an additional 1.0 to 2.0°C rise above these figures, severely straining power grids and public health systems.

2. The "Passive First" Philosophy

The core of the roadmap mandates a paradigm shift in building architecture. This section outlines the sequential design hierarchy required to break the cycle of mechanical dependency. By prioritizing lean architecture and passive interventions, developers can reduce capital costs and minimize operational energy before specifying any active cooling systems.

1. LEAN ARCHITECTURE

Optimize building orientation, urban morphology, and spatial layouts to minimize baseline heat gain at near-zero incremental capital cost.

↓

2. PASSIVE DESIGN

Deploy high-performance envelopes, natural ventilation, targeted shading, cool roofs, and insulation to reduce residual cooling loads to the absolute minimum.

↓

3. EFFICIENT ACTIVE SYSTEMS

Size and deploy mechanical cooling (AC and fans) only to address the remaining load, avoiding over-specification and grid stress.

3. Technology & Design Matrix

This section evaluates specific passive cooling interventions applicable to the ASEAN context. It provides a comparative analysis of cooling energy savings, allowing stakeholders to select the most cost-effective and climate-appropriate strategies for new constructions and retrofits.

⚙ Cooling Energy Savings Potential

Percentage ranges of cooling energy savings derived from different passive technologies.

🏘

Cool Roofs

Excellent region-wide suitability. Maximizes albedo. Best return for low-income communities. Payback: 3-5 years.

💨

Passive Ventilation

High potential in monsoon seasons. Constrained by urban noise/pollution. Payback: 6-9 years.

📝

Envelope Optimization

Essential intervention. Low-E glazing (SHGC ≤ 0.25) is high-impact in dense cities. Payback: 5-10 years.
